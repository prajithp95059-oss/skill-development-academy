Our story

Started by people tired of the gap.

Stackly began as a weekend mentoring circle for a handful of self-taught developers. It's now a six-track academy — but the format never really changed.

How it started

A mentoring circle that outgrew a living room

In 2021, three engineers started reviewing portfolio projects for friends who were stuck in application black holes — talented, self-taught, and unable to get past a resume filter. The reviews worked. People started getting interviews.

By 2023 the circle had a curriculum, a cohort structure, and a name. Today it's Stackly's Skill Development Academy — same mentor-review model, just built to run at scale.

What we believe

The four rules the academy still runs on

These haven't changed since the living-room days — they're the reason the format scales without losing what made it work.

01
Proof

Show the work, not a grade

A certificate doesn't get anyone hired. A portfolio a mentor has actually reviewed does — so that's what every track produces.

02
People

A human reviews every brief

No AI grading, no rubric-only scoring. Every submission gets read and annotated by someone doing the job today.

03
Access

Talent shouldn't need a filter

The whole company started because self-taught developers were losing to resume filters, not to skill. That's still the problem we're solving.

04
Scale, carefully

Grow the model, not around it

Every new track keeps the same mentor-ratio and review loop as the original cohort — growth doesn't get to dilute that.
